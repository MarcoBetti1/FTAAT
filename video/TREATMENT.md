# GPT Learning — Episode 01

Working title: **We gave AI a bigger desk. It still lost the paperwork.**
Alternative if models ace the tests: **We tried to make AI lose one fact. Our test broke first.**
Do not choose the final title until the evidence supports it.

## Personality

An absurdly tidy experimental office: the Bureau of Misplaced Facts. The host is
curious, dryly amused, and willing to discover that our own test is wrong. A small
paper clerk, Pip, tries to sort an avalanche of record cards. Pip represents the task,
not a faithful visualization of how a transformer thinks.

Visual language: warm ivory, midnight ink, mustard, and mint. Large mathematical
labels, card shuffles, moving bookmarks, clean chart reveals. One visual idea at a
time. Original drawings and diagrams; no imitation of another channel's mascot,
voice, intro, or signature composition. Inspiration is explanatory clarity and
visual reasoning, with more playful pauses and small bureaucratic jokes.

## 7–9 minute story after data collection

- 0:00 Cold open: a real, reproducible surprising outcome, then show the exact question.
  If no failures occur, the surprise is how well the models do.
- 0:25 Establish context as a desk that holds supplied text. The desk-size analogy
  describes capacity, not a sequential scanning mechanism or persistent memory.
- 0:55 Token ruler: the same sentence is cut differently by different tokenizers.
  Show actual tokenization where a local tokenizer exists; label conceptual cuts
  explicitly otherwise. Explain why letters, symbols and model tokens are different.
- 1:35 Game 1: one missing file among many. Ask viewers to predict the effect of
  moving it to the middle. Reveal per-position results with sample counts.
- 2:45 Game 2: follow two arrows. Show exactly the two evidence records used in a
  real trial. Reveal the model answer only after the viewer can solve it.
- 3:45 Game 3: stale paperwork. Revision 9 beats revision 2 even if revision 2 is last.
  "The filing cabinet has discovered bureaucracy."
- 4:45 Game 4: the entire inventory. Explain that this also tests long answer production.
  An output cap is a full printer tray, not proof that information vanished.
- 5:45 Comparison: older/cheaper versus newer models, separately from inference effort.
  Use real usage and conservative costs. Show uncertainty; avoid a universal winner.
- 6:45 Test the test: original FTAAT grading bug, why prefix accuracy lied, and how we
  fixed it. This is an honest methodological reveal, not a manufactured scandal.
- 7:30 What the data can and cannot tell us; link complete methodology and code.
  End on a precise next question, potentially chess state tracking in a later episode.
